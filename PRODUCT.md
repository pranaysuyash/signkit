## Platform

web

## Users

The public landing page is a B2C acquisition surface for individuals who need to
extract a handwritten signature from a scan, photo, or document and place it on a
PDF without handing sensitive files to a cloud service. The broader product also
serves repeated, high-consequence Legal, HR, and document-operations workflows;
that depth is real product context, not an excuse for enterprise-style marketing.

## Product Purpose

SignKit is a local-first desktop workflow for extracting, cleaning, storing, and
placing signatures on PDFs. The immediate visitor outcome is a credible path from
"this signature is trapped in a document" to a clean, usable PDF signature.

## Positioning

Unlike browser-first signing services or generic PDF suites, SignKit keeps the
signature extraction and PDF-placement workflow on the user’s computer by default.
It combines a focused, personal utility experience with the foundations for
controlled signed-document work.

## Operating Context

Visitors arrive with sensitive or time-sensitive documents, usually on desktop.
They need a clear demonstration of the extract → clean → place workflow, a reason
to trust local processing, and an honest purchase route. The current launch offer
is $29, rising to a $39 one-time Personal license, with a 30-day refund policy.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Extract and clean signatures from supported documents and images.
- Keep a local signature Vault and place signatures on PDFs.
- Core processing is local by default; the product must not imply silent cloud
  upload or a regulated-signature guarantee.
- Current desktop direction also includes precise PDF placement and reusable
  placement/template primitives. Controlled local packet work exists as an
  in-development product surface with recipes, scoped grants, input/output/review
  folders, review, retry, and quarantine. It is not a public purchase promise.
- Certificate-backed or regulated signatures, browser-native cloud workspaces,
  hybrid synchronization, and external-system adapters are future research
  horizons. They must be presented as such until their data boundaries,
  permissions, recovery paths, and compliance evidence are complete.
- The landing prototype must preserve accessible navigation, keyboard focus,
  responsive layout, and a reduced-motion experience.
- Checkout configuration is not currently production-complete: the Dodo product
  ID is empty and Gumroad is the configured fallback. A concept CTA therefore
  remains a non-purchasing preview action.

## Brand Commitments

SignKit is polished, current B2C software, not a generic enterprise dashboard.
The public surface may be expressive and tactile, but it must remain precise,
privacy-literate, and free of invented social proof or technical claims.

## Product Principles

1. Make the local workflow tangible before asking for a purchase.
2. Treat privacy as a visible product behavior, not a decorative badge.
3. Keep one-time ownership clear without fake urgency or unverified comparisons.
4. Let B2C craft make a serious document task feel immediate and capable.
5. Never overstate what a placed signature means legally or operationally.
6. Give distinct user jobs distinct visual postures rather than stretching one
   landing-page aesthetic across personal, precision, and operational work.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA visual and interaction basics: semantic landmarks, contrast,
visible focus, target size, and support for the operating-system reduced-motion
preference.
